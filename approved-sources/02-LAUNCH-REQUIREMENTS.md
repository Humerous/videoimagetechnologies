# 1. APPROVED VIT POSITIONING

VIT must be presented as broader than film and production.

Primary emphasis:

**DATA → FILES → INDEXING → STORAGE → ARCHIVE → VERIFICATION → AUDITING → WORKFLOW → SYSTEMS RELIABILITY → RECOVERY → HANDOVER**

Film, television and production remain important specialist capabilities that VIT can facilitate, but they must not define the whole company.

The website must clearly serve clients with indexing, filing, archive, verification, auditing, storage, workflow, recovery and systems requirements even where those clients have nothing to do with film production.

Preserve:

**Clear Vision. Reliable Systems.**

**Media · Data · Systems Architecture.**

VIT should not be positioned primarily as a film production company.

## 2.3 DATA RECOVERY & SUPPORT

Controlled process:

**INTAKE → ASSESS → AUTHORISE → PROTECT SOURCE → RECOVER → VERIFY → ORGANISE → SECURE HANDOVER**

Includes:

- Drive/data assessment
- Recovery feasibility
- Source scanning/review
- Controlled recovery
- File retrieval attempts
- Structure preservation where possible
- Verification/QC
- Recovery reporting
- Secure handover
- Specialist escalation where required

Requirements:

- VIT must not guarantee successful recovery.
- Source media must be protected from unnecessary modification or additional stress.
- Billable recovery work must operate against defined scope and client authorisation.
- Recovery outcomes should be verified and documented where practical.
- Temporary working/recovered data must have a defined secure handling, retention and deletion process.
- VIT must distinguish work within its own technical capability from cases requiring specialist physical-drive or laboratory recovery.
- No specialist cleanroom, forensic, hardware-repair, certification, success-rate or laboratory capability may be claimed unless verified.

Recovery intake should capture enough information to understand:

- Device/media type
- Capacity
- Failure/event
- Current accessibility
- Previous recovery attempts
- Encryption
- Urgency
- Confidential/sensitive handling requirements
- Potential legal relevance where applicable

# 11. LEGAL / PRIVACY / LIABILITY STANDARD

VIT must be structured for the highest practical level of legal, privacy, contractual and operational protection available for its services.

The system must be designed to:

- Protect VIT's rights
- Protect client rights
- Protect client data
- Establish clear authority before work begins
- Establish clear scope and exclusions
- Record client approvals
- Limit unnecessary access to client systems and information
- Control copying, modification, recovery and deletion of data
- Maintain confidentiality
- Maintain evidence of work performed
- Define responsibility and liability boundaries
- Define retention and secure deletion
- Protect VIT against unauthorised instructions or scope creep
- Provide defensible records if a dispute occurs

Required protection layers:

- POPIA compliance framework
- PAIA compliance
- Information Officer status confirmed
- Privacy Notice
- Website Terms of Use
- Client Terms & Conditions
- Service-specific scopes
- Written client authority
- Access Authorisation
- Data Handling Authorisation
- Recovery Authorisation
- Separate approval for destructive actions
- Confidentiality provisions
- Data retention and secure-deletion rules
- Security incident / data breach procedure
- Device intake and handover records where relevant
- Limitation-of-liability provisions where legally enforceable
- Appropriate indemnities where legally enforceable
- Equipment Rental Terms
- Appropriate business insurance
- Legal review by a South African commercial/privacy lawyer before higher-risk services are commercially launched

Recovery engagements must clearly record:

- Who owns or controls the data
- Who authorised VIT
- Device condition at intake
- What VIT is permitted to do
- What VIT is not permitted to do
- Known risks
- Previous recovery attempts
- Whether destructive/invasive work is permitted
- Recovery scope
- Client approval
- Recovery result
- Verification performed
- Handover
- Working-copy retention/deletion

Rule:

The target is maximum practical legal protection and defensibility.

VIT must not claim that legal action is impossible or that liability can be eliminated completely.

Normal enquiries must not automatically subscribe visitors to marketing communications.

Cookie / consent controls are conditional on the technologies actually deployed.

Legal and privacy wording must reflect VIT's real operation and website implementation and must not rely on generic copied boilerplate.
